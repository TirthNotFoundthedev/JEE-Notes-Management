Mechanical Properties of Matter

Comprehensive review of solids and fluids based on chapter curriculum.

1 Stress: The Internal Resistance

When a body is subjected to a deforming force, a restoring force is developed in the body. Stress is defined as the restoring force per unit area.

\[ Stress = \frac{Force (F)}{Area (A)} \]
  • SI Unit: \(N m^{-2}\) or Pascal (Pa)
  • Dimensional Formula: \([ML^{-1}T^{-2}]\)

Tensile & Compressive

Longitudinal stress. Tensile increases length; Compressive decreases it.

Tangential (Shearing)

Forces applied parallel to the cross-section, causing relative displacement between faces.

2 Volume Stress (Hydraulic Stress)

When an object is submerged in a fluid, the force applied by the fluid acts perpendicularly at each point. This is Hydraulic Stress.

\[ Hydraulic Stress = \text{Hydraulic Pressure (P)} = \frac{F}{A} \]

It leads to a decrease in volume without changing the geometrical shape.

3 Strain: The Deformation Ratio

Strain is the ratio of change in dimension to the original dimension. It is dimensionless and has no units.

Longitudinal Strain: \( \epsilon = \frac{\Delta L}{L} \)

Shearing Strain: \( \theta = \frac{\Delta x}{L} \approx \tan \theta \)

Volume Strain: \( \frac{\Delta V}{V} \)

4 Hooke's Law & Moduli of Elasticity

Hooke's Law: For small deformations, stress is directly proportional to strain.

\[ Stress \propto Strain \implies Stress = k \times Strain \]

The Elastic Moduli

Modulus Formula Significance
Young's (Y) \( Y = \frac{FL}{A\Delta L} \) Resistance to length change (Solids only).
Shear (G) \( G = \frac{F}{A\theta} \) Resistance to shape change (Rigidity).
Bulk (B) \( B = \frac{-P}{\Delta V/V} \) Resistance to volume change.

Compressibility (k): Reciprocal of Bulk Modulus. \( k = 1/B \). Solids are least compressible; Gases are most.

Poisson's Ratio (\( \sigma \)): \( \sigma = \frac{\text{Lateral Strain}}{\text{Longitudinal Strain}} = \frac{\Delta d/d}{\Delta L/L} \).

5 Relation between Longitudinal Stress & Strain

Metal Wire (Ductile)

Metal obeys Hooke's law until the Proportional Limit. Beyond the Yield Point, it deforms plastically.

Rubber (Elastomer)

Rubber can be stretched to large strains but does not obey Hooke's law. It returns to original shape but lacks a linear region.

6 Elastic Potential Energy

Work done against inter-atomic forces during deformation is stored as potential energy.

\[ W = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Young's Modulus} \times (\text{Strain})^2 \times \text{Volume} \] \[ \text{Energy density (u)} = \frac{1}{2} \times \text{Stress} \times \text{Strain} \]

7 Determination of Young's Modulus in Lab

Typically measured using Searle's Apparatus. It uses two wires (Reference and Experimental) to eliminate errors from support yielding or temperature changes.

  • Calculate cross-section area \( A = \pi r^2 \).
  • Measure original length \( L \).
  • Add weights to experimental wire and measure extension \( \Delta L \) using a spherometer/vernier scale.
  • Plot \( M \) vs \( \Delta L \); slope gives Young's modulus.

8 Surface Tension

Surface tension (\( S \)) is the force per unit length acting in the plane of the interface between the liquid and the bounding surface.

\[ S = \frac{Force (F)}{Length (L)} \]

Unit: \( N/m \). Dimensional formula: \([MT^{-2}]\).

9 Surface Energy

The work done to increase the surface area of a liquid. Molecules on the surface have more potential energy than those in the bulk.

\[ \text{Surface Energy} = S \times \Delta A \]

10 Excess Pressure

Surface tension causes pressure inside a curved surface to be higher than outside.

Liquid Drop

One surface interface.

\( P_2 - P_1 = \frac{2S}{R} \)

Soap Bubble

Two surface interfaces (inner & outer).

\( P_2 - P_1 = \frac{4S}{R} \)

13 Contact Angle (\( \theta \))

The angle between the tangent to the liquid surface at the point of contact and the solid surface inside the liquid.

  • \( \theta < 90^\circ \) (Acute): Liquid "wets" the surface (e.g., Water on glass).
  • \( \theta > 90^\circ \) (Obtuse): Liquid does not wet the surface (e.g., Mercury on glass).

14 Capillary Rise (Ascent Formula)

The rise or fall of a liquid in a narrow tube (capillary) is due to surface tension.

\[ h = \frac{2S \cos \theta}{r \rho g} \]

Where \( r \) is tube radius, \( \rho \) is density, and \( g \) is gravity.

15 Viscosity

The resistance offered by a fluid to its own flow (internal friction). Defined by Newton's law of viscosity:

\[ F = -\eta A \frac{dv}{dx} \]

Where \( \eta \) is the Coefficient of Viscosity. Unit: Poiseuille (Pl) or \( Pa \cdot s \).

16 Flow through Narrow Tubes & Stokes Law

Poiseuille's Equation

Volume flow rate (\( V \)) through a narrow tube of length \( L \) and radius \( r \):

\[ V = \frac{\pi P r^4}{8 \eta L} \]

Stokes' Law

Viscous drag force (\( F \)) on a sphere of radius \( r \) moving with velocity \( v \):

\[ F = 6 \pi \eta r v \]

Terminal Velocity (\( v_t \))

The constant velocity reached by a falling body when viscous force + buoyant force = weight.

\[ v_t = \frac{2}{9} \frac{r^2 (\rho - \sigma) g}{\eta} \]

(\( \rho \) = density of object, \( \sigma \) = density of fluid)

Reynolds Number (\( R_e \))

Determines the type of flow (Laminar vs Turbulent).

\[ R_e = \frac{\rho v d}{\eta} \]
  • \( R_e < 1000 \): Laminar Flow
  • \( R_e > 2000 \): Turbulent Flow
